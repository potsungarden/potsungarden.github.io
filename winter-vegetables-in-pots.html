Winter vegetables in pots: six easy crops for an autumn and winter balcony

PotsunGarden · Updated 20 October 2026

Six easy vegetables for pots over winter are rocket, cut-and-come-again lettuce, radishes, baby spinach, mizuna and pea shoots. In mild to cool temperate climates, sow them in October and early November, in pots 15–20 cm deep (10–15 cm for pea shoots) with 3–4 hours of direct sun or more (radishes like 4–6). First harvests come after about 3–8 weeks, and growth almost stops in December and January. Here is what to sow, and what honestly to expect over winter.

First, your climate

This advice is for mild to cool temperate climates, such as much of Italy, the UK and the mid-latitude US (roughly USDA zones 7–10). In colder places, or where hard frosts are common, sow early in October, keep pots in the most sheltered spot by the wall, and cover them with fleece on frosty nights.

In mild-winter climates (for example the Mediterranean coasts of Spain, Portugal, Italy and Greece, Southern California, Florida, or Sydney, Brisbane and Perth), autumn is actually the start of the main salad season. In the southern hemisphere, read the months six months later: autumn is March to May.

These are all cool-season crops. Colorado State University Extension notes that cool-season vegetables prefer cool growing temperatures and are often replanted in midsummer for an autumn harvest; lettuce, spinach, peas and radish are in its "hardy" group.

A few crop notes

  • Rocket: the RHS says wild rocket may keep cropping into winter if protected, and starts again in early spring. Salad rocket is quicker but shorter-lived.
  • Spinach: choose a variety sold for autumn or winter sowing, ideally mildew-resistant. The RHS recommends hardy varieties for overwintering, covered with cloches or fleece from October unless your climate is mild. Autumn-sown spinach may flower in spring as the days lengthen; pick the last leaves and sow again.
  • Radishes: best sown early in autumn. Late sowings on a dim balcony tend to make leaves and no roots, so if your winter sun is poor, wait and sow again in February.
  • Mizuna: a feathery Japanese leaf with a mild mustard flavour, very cold-hardy. Cut at about 10 cm and it regrows. Birds love it in winter, so drape fine netting over a few bent wires.
  • Pea shoots: soak edible garden pea seed for 8–12 hours, sow close together and cut at 10–15 cm. Never use sweet peas: they are an ornamental flower and poisonous.

Check your sun again

The autumn sun is lower than in summer, so shadows are longer and your balcony may look very different. Watch it for two days and count the hours of direct sun. Three to four hours suits most leafy greens; radishes like 4–6.

How often should you water pots in winter?

Pots dry slowly in autumn and winter, so you will water far less than in summer, often only once or twice a week. Use the finger test and water in the morning.

Overwatering is the bigger risk now. The RHS names overwatering as the most common cause of losing container plants, and its winter advice is to remove saucers, move pots out of the rain if the mix becomes sodden, and avoid watering when frost is forecast. If a pot stays wet for days, clear the drainage hole and raise it on pot feet.

How do you protect pots from frost?

  • Group pots close together near the house wall. The RHS notes this helps protect roots from cold in winter.
  • Keep fleece ready for frosty nights. The RHS says heavier grades of fleece give about 2 °C of protection.
  • Do not touch frozen leaves. Many recover once they thaw.

What to expect, month by month

  • October: fresh sun check, fill pots with fresh mix, sow rocket, lettuce, radishes, spinach and mizuna, and a first pot of pea shoots.
  • November: last outdoor sowings (rocket, mizuna, pea shoots), thin seedlings and eat the thinnings, first small harvests.
  • December and January: almost no growth in the short days, and that is normal. Pick lightly and leave the centres. The RHS notes feeding container plants is generally not needed in winter.
  • February: growth starts again as the days lengthen. Sow spinach and lettuce for spring, and radishes and rocket late in the month (under fleece in cold areas).

A small bowl of leaves from a few pots in December is a real harvest. Do not expect supermarket quantities; do expect fresh, peppery leaves you grew yourself.

Frequently asked questions

What vegetables can I grow in pots over winter?

In mild to cool temperate climates, easy choices are rocket, cut-and-come-again lettuce, radishes, baby spinach, mizuna and pea shoots, sown in October and early November (March to May in the southern hemisphere).

Do pots need watering in winter?

Yes, but much less. Check with the finger test and water in the morning when the mix feels dry. Empty or remove saucers, and avoid watering when frost is forecast.

How do I protect balcony pots from frost?

Group pots together near the house wall, keep garden fleece ready for frosty nights, and move pots out of heavy rain so the mix does not get sodden. Do not handle frozen leaves; many recover after they thaw.

Why have my winter salads stopped growing?

In December and January the days are too short for much growth. That is normal. Pick lightly, leave the centres, and growth will start again in February.

Can I grow pea shoots indoors in winter?

Yes. Sow soaked edible pea seed in a pot 10–15 cm deep on your brightest windowsill and cut the shoots at 10–15 cm, usually after about 3–6 weeks. Never use sweet pea seed, which is poisonous.
