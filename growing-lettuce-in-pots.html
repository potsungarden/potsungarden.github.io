Growing lettuce in pots: cut-and-come-again salad on a balcony

PotsunGarden · Updated 20 October 2026

To grow lettuce in a pot, sow loose-leaf lettuce thinly in a pot of at least 8 litres (2 gallons) with 15 cm (6 in) of potting mix, in a spot with 3–6 hours of direct sun. Start picking when the leaves are about 10 cm (4 in) long, usually 4–6 weeks after sowing (about 5–8 weeks in autumn). Pick the outer leaves, or cut the whole pot 2–3 cm (1 in) above the base, and it grows again: one trough can give a small salad every week for several weeks.

What "cut-and-come-again" means

Instead of growing one big lettuce head and cutting it once, you grow loose-leaf lettuce or a salad-leaf mix and pick the leaves while the plant keeps growing. The RHS describes loose-leaf types as quicker than hearting lettuces, with first leaves often ready about a month after sowing and pickings for several weeks after that. They also need very little room, which is why they suit pots.

What pot, mix and sun does lettuce need?

  • Pot: at least 8 litres (2 gallons) with 15 cm (6 in) of mix. A trough is ideal. For a sowing of several plants, the RHS suggests a container at least 30 cm (1 ft) wide.
  • Mix: bagged potting mix (multi-purpose compost in the UK), not garden soil.
  • Sun: 3–6 hours of direct sun. In summer, give it afternoon shade: heat makes lettuce bolt.
  • Season: spring and autumn are best; in summer, grow it in shade.

How do you sow lettuce in a pot?

  1. Fill the pot to 2–3 cm (1 in) below the rim and water the mix until it is evenly damp. Let it drain.
  2. Sprinkle the seed thinly over the surface.
  3. Barely cover it with a dusting of mix. Lettuce seed needs a little light to germinate.
  4. Keep the surface damp, not wet, until the seedlings appear, usually in 4–10 days.

Lettuce can fail to come up in hot weather. The RHS notes that high temperatures can stop some varieties germinating, so in hot spells sow in the evening, water with cold water and keep the pot in shade until the seedlings appear.

Seeds do not keep well. The RHS recommends buying fresh lettuce seed each year for reliable germination; check the packing date on the packet.

Spacing and thinning

For full-size loose-leaf plants, aim for about 15 cm (6 in) between plants, or 3–4 plants in a 30 cm (12 in) pot. For baby leaves you can leave them closer. Thin when seedlings have their first true leaves, snipping the extras at soil level with scissors so you do not disturb the neighbours' roots. The thinnings are good to eat.

How do you cut lettuce so it regrows?

Start picking when the leaves are about 10 cm (4 in) long, usually 4–6 weeks after sowing (slower in autumn: allow about 5–8 weeks). You have two options:

  • Pick the outer leaves from every plant and leave the centre to keep growing.
  • Cut the whole pot 2–3 cm (1 in) above the base with clean scissors. It usually regrows once or twice.

Take a few leaves from each plant rather than stripping one. Harvest in the morning if you can, when leaves are fresh.

Watering and feeding

Use the finger test: push a finger 2–3 cm (1 in) into the mix. If it is dry at that depth, water until water runs out of the holes; if it is still damp, wait and check tomorrow. Water in the morning, at the base. The RHS advises avoiding evening watering because overnight damp attracts slugs and snails and encourages fungal disease.

Fresh bagged mix feeds plants for about five to six weeks. After that, give leafy crops a balanced liquid feed every two weeks, at the dilution on the label.

Sow a little, often

A single sowing runs out in a few weeks. For a steady supply, sow a third of a trough now, another third about three weeks later, and the last third three weeks after that. By the time the first third bolts, the next one is ready.

Common problems

Bolting. A stalk shoots up from the middle and the leaves turn bitter. Heat, dry mix and long days set it off. Pick the whole pot, then sow again in light shade, and keep the mix evenly moist from the start. Varieties sold as slow to bolt help.

Slugs and snails. Ragged holes and silvery trails. Check pots with a torch on damp evenings and remove them by hand; look under pot rims too. Raising pots on pot feet helps.

Easy varieties for pots

  • 'Salad Bowl': green or red oak-leaf type, slow to bolt.
  • 'Lollo Rosso': frilly red leaves, pretty in a mixed pot.
  • 'Black Seeded Simpson': fast, pale green, a US classic.
  • Any salad-leaf mix or mesclun, for a ready-made mixed bowl. For autumn sowing, look for a mix sold for autumn or winter.

Frequently asked questions

How long does lettuce take to grow in a pot?

Loose-leaf lettuce is usually ready to pick 4–6 weeks after sowing in mild weather. In autumn, allow about 5–8 weeks for the first cut.

How many times can you cut lettuce and it grows back?

If you cut the whole pot 2–3 cm (1 in) above the base, it usually regrows once or twice. If you pick only the outer leaves, you can keep picking for several weeks until the plants bolt.

How deep should a pot be for lettuce?

At least 15 cm (6 in) of potting mix. A trough is ideal, and the RHS suggests a container at least 30 cm (1 ft) wide for a sowing.

Why is my lettuce bitter?

It has probably bolted: heat, dry mix and long days make lettuce send up a flower stalk, and the leaves turn bitter. Pick what is left, then sow again in light shade and keep the mix evenly moist.

Can lettuce grow in shade on a balcony?

Lettuce manages with 3–4 hours of direct sun, and in summer it actually prefers afternoon shade. Below about 3 hours it grows slowly.
